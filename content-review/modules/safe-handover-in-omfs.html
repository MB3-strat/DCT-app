Safe Handover in OMFS

The quality of your handover is often the quality of your patient's overnight care.

#handover#patient-safety#on-call#communication#escalation#professionalism
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

Handover is one of the highest-risk moments in healthcare. Critical information can be lost, assumptions can be made and important tasks may be overlooked. Many serious incidents are linked not to poor clinical care, but to poor communication during transitions between teams. A safe handover ensures the incoming team understands who the patients are, why they are under OMFS, who is clinically unwell, what has been done, what still needs to happen, and what they should be concerned about overnight. Your responsibility does not end when your shift finishes—it ends when care has been safely transferred.

Golden Rule

Never hand over a task that you could reasonably complete before the end of your shift. Outstanding jobs should be the exception, not the rule.

A Structured Handover: I-PASS

Prioritising Patients at Handover

Priority 1 – Immediate Attention: Airway compromise, active haemorrhage, septic patient, post-op deterioration, expanding neck swelling. Discuss first.

Priority 2 – Requires Review Overnight: Awaiting CT, repeat bloods, drain monitoring, facial swelling reassessment, post-op review.

Priority 3 – Routine Tasks: Discharge paperwork, prescriptions, clinic bookings, follow-up. Only hand over if genuinely unavoidable.

What Must Never Be Omitted

For every patient: working diagnosis, current observations, airway status, recent deterioration, pending investigations, outstanding treatment, senior involvement, escalation plan. If discussed with registrar/consultant, say so.

Red Flags ⚠️

Never leave without discussing: actual/potential airway compromise, active bleeding or high bleeding risk, septic or deteriorating, awaiting emergency surgery or returned from emergency theatre, unresolved diagnostic uncertainty requiring overnight reassessment.

DCT Handover Checklist

Before leaving shift, ensure incoming doctor knows:

✓Patient name and location
✓Working diagnosis
✓Current clinical status
✓Airway assessment
✓Outstanding investigations
✓Outstanding tasks
✓Senior discussions
✓Escalation plan
✓Patients requiring overnight review
✓Patients expected back from theatre

Clearly distinguish between essential tasks and those that can wait

Highlight trends rather than isolated results — rising CRP or worsening trismus matters

Prioritise clinically unstable patients first — they get discussed first

Clearly communicate outstanding tasks, anticipated problems and escalation plans

Never leave until confident responsibility has been safely handed over

Registrar Handover Prompt

When giving handover, start with sickest patients. For each: one-line summary, current status, specific jobs, specific triggers for escalation: "Please review immediately if mouth opening deteriorates below 15mm or new dysphagia develops."
