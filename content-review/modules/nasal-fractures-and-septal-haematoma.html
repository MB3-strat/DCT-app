Nasal Fractures and Septal Haematoma

A nasal fracture rarely threatens the patient. A missed septal haematoma can permanently damage the nose. Always look inside before looking outside.

#nasal-fracture#septal-haematoma#ENT#facial-trauma#epistaxis#saddle-nose-deformity#red-flag#on-call
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

Nasal fractures are the commonest facial bone fracture. Most are isolated, low-energy injuries managed in outpatients. They rarely cause airway compromise or life-threatening bleeding. However, one complication must never be missed: septal haematoma. If unrecognised, it leads to septal cartilage necrosis, septal perforation, and saddle-nose deformity — a permanent cosmetic and functional problem. The OMFS DCT must differentiate simple nasal fracture from injuries requiring urgent drainage, and screen for associated facial/C-spine/head injuries. Your job: control bleeding, exclude septal haematoma, document function, safety-net, and refer appropriately.

Golden Rule

Always look inside the nose. The septum matters more than the dorsum. A missed septal haematoma is indefensible.

Initial Assessment – ATLS Principles

Nasal fractures are usually isolated, but high-energy mechanisms require full trauma assessment.

Airway: Usually patent. Severe bilateral fractures + gross swelling can compromise airway. Blood in oropharynx → nausea/vomiting.

Breathing: RR, SpO2. Chest injuries if high-energy.

Circulation: Profuse epistaxis can cause hypotension, especially elderly/anticoagulated. Control bleeding first.

Disability: GCS, pupils, LOC, vomiting, amnesia. Head injury common with nasal #.

Exposure: Check for other facial lacerations, dental trauma, ocular injuries, C-spine tenderness.

History

Mechanism: Punch, fall, sport, RTC, assault. High-energy = screen for other injuries.

Nasal symptoms: Epistaxis — how long, one or both nostrils, soaked towels? Nasal obstruction — immediate or delayed? Change in shape — patient often best judge.

Pain: Over bridge, worse on palpation.

Previous nasal injury/surgery: Baseline deformity? Previous septoplasty/rhinoplasty affects management.

Associated: Visual disturbance, diplopia, infraorbital numbness, dental pain, CSF rhinorrhoea.

Examination

External: Swelling, bruising, lacerations, deviation, dorsal depression, telecanthus. Assess from front and bird’s-eye view. Palpate: tenderness, crepitus, step deformity, mobility. Check bony and cartilaginous dorsum.

Internal: Essential. Use headlight + Thudicum speculum or otoscope. Inspect septum for:

Septal haematoma: Bilateral, boggy, bluish swelling of septum. Soft fluctuant mass occluding airway. May be subtle early.

Septal deviation: New vs old.

Lacerations: Source of bleeding.

Foreign body: Tooth fragments, glass.

Other: Check eyes — VA, pupils, EOM, diplopia. Check occlusion — nasal # rarely alone if high-energy. Check infraorbital nerve.

Septal Haematoma – The Must-Not-Miss

Pathophysiology: Blood collects between septal cartilage and mucoperichondrium → pressure necrosis of avascular cartilage → collapse in 3–7 days → saddle-nose deformity + septal perforation.

Signs: Nasal obstruction out of proportion to external swelling, bilateral soft swelling of septum, may be red/blue. Unilateral can occur but rare.

Management: Needs urgent drainage + nasal packing + antibiotics. ENT emergency if OMFS not comfortable. Do not discharge a septal haematoma.

Red flag phrase: “My nose feels blocked and it’s getting worse.”

Epistaxis Control Most stops with simple measures:

Sit patient forward, pinch soft part of nose 10–15 min.

Ice pack to bridge.

If fails: topical vasoconstrictor + LA spray, silver nitrate cautery if bleeding point seen.

Anterior nasal pack: Merocel/RapidRhino. Bilateral if needed.

Posterior pack or Foley catheter if posterior bleed.

Consider coagulopathy, anticoagulants, BP control.

ENT/OMFS if uncontrolled, balloon tamponade, IR embolisation, theatre.

Red Flags 🚩

Urgent senior/ENT review if: septal haematoma, uncontrolled epistaxis, CSF rhinorrhoea, decreased VA, diplopia, enophthalmos, suspected orbital injury, telecanthus, C-spine concerns, severe deformity in child, open fracture with exposed cartilage/bone.

Investigations

X-rays: Not routinely required for simple nasal fractures. Poor sensitivity, do not change management.

CT Facial Bones: If suspected other facial fractures, severe deformity, high-energy mechanism, orbital signs, CSF leak, child with significant trauma.

Photographs: Medicolegal + baseline. Always take with consent if deformity present.

Management Isolated Nasal Fracture, No Septal Haematoma:

Acute: Control bleeding, analgesia, ice, head elevation. Advise against nose blowing. Most not reduced acutely due to swelling.

Referral: ENT/OMFS clinic 5–7 days when swelling settles. MUA ± septoplasty if functional/cosmetic deformity. Patient decides.

Antibiotics: Not routine. Consider if open wound, packing in situ, or per local protocol.

Tetanus: Update if dirty wound.

Septal Haematoma:

Urgent drainage: Needle aspiration or incision + drainage under LA.

Packing: Bilateral nasal packs to prevent re-accumulation.

Antibiotics: Cover Staph/Strep per local guidelines due to risk of abscess.

Admit: Usually 24–48h observation. ENT follow-up.

Discharge Advice

No contact sports 6 weeks.

No nose blowing 2 weeks.

Sleep head elevated.

Analgesia.

Return if: worsening nasal obstruction, fever, vision changes, severe headache, clear nasal discharge, recurrent bleeding.

Warn swelling will worsen days 1–3 before improving.

Explain that final cosmetic result assessed after swelling settles — 7–10 days.

Admission Criteria

Rare. Consider if: uncontrolled epistaxis, septal haematoma, associated facial/head injury, airway concerns, social concerns, paediatric with significant trauma, need for GA.

Key Takeaways

Always examine inside the nose — septal haematoma is the complication that matters

Epistaxis management: pressure, cautery, pack, escalate. Don’t send home bleeding

Safety-net: worsening obstruction, vision changes, CSF leak, fever → return

Registrar Handover Prompt

Structure: Mechanism, epistaxis controlled?, septal haematoma excluded?, deformity, other injuries, plan.

“22M punched. No LOC, C-spine cleared. Epistaxis stopped with 10min pressure. No septal haematoma on speculum exam. External swelling, dorsal deviation R. VA normal, no diplopia. No other facial #. Plan: discharge with nasal fracture advice, ENT clinic 1/52, safety-netted.”

Red flag version: “5yo fall. Nasal obstruction worsening. Bilateral boggy septum — septal haematoma. Needs ENT now for drainage.”

Exclude septal haematoma — use headlight + speculum

Document vision — even if “unrelated”

Miss septal haematoma and the patient gets a permanent deformity. Miss bleeding and they re-attend. Miss vision and you miss orbital injury.
