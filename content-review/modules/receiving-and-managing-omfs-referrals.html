Receiving and Managing OMFS Referrals

A good referral is the beginning of good patient care. Your job is to establish why the patient has been referred, determine how urgently they need to be seen, and ensure they receive the right care at the right time.

#referrals#on-call#triage#airway#communication#escalation#patient-safety
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

Every on-call shift revolves around referrals. Some require immediate intervention, while others can safely wait. The ability to receive a structured referral, identify red flags, prioritise competing demands and communicate effectively with both referrers and senior colleagues is one of the defining skills of a competent OMFS DCT. Never accept a referral without understanding exactly what is being asked of you.

Golden Rule

Never start by asking, "Which tooth?" Start by asking: "Is the patient stable?" The airway and physiological status always come before the dental diagnosis.

The OMFS Referral Framework: REFERRAL

Prioritising Referrals

Immediate: Airway compromise, active haemorrhage, orbital compartment syndrome, deep neck infection, septic patient, expanding neck swelling — discuss with registrar without delay.

Urgent: Mandibular fracture, facial laceration, dental trauma, facial cellulitis, suspected orbital fracture — usually within one hour.

Routine: Dental pain, dry socket, chronic TMJ pain, oral ulcer, post-op review — can wait until higher priority patients reviewed.

Neuro: reduced GCS, focal neurology with facial injury

DCT Checklist: Before Ending Any Referral

✓Do I understand why OMFS has been asked to review?
✓Is the airway safe?
✓How urgent is this?
✓Have I informed my registrar if necessary?
✓Does the patient require admission?
✓Have I documented the referral?

Every referral deserves courtesy, even if ultimately inappropriate

Clarify the clinical question before attending. "Please review" is not a diagnosis

Repeat observations yourself if the patient appears unwell

If you think the referral is inappropriate, discuss professionally and explain reasoning. Never dismiss concerns without assessment

Airway, haemorrhage and sepsis take precedence over all other referrals

Good communication with referrers and seniors improves patient safety and workflow

Structured approach makes on-call safer, more efficient, less stressful

Registrar Handover Prompt

Use SBAR. Situation: age, sex, location, presenting problem. Background: relevant PMH. Assessment: history, exam, obs, imaging. Recommendation: "I think..." "I'd like...". Before calling know: airway status, NEWS2, source tooth if swelling, mouth opening, dysphagia, bloods, imaging, working diagnosis, proposed plan.
