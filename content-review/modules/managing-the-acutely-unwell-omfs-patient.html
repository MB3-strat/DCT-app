Managing the Acutely Unwell OMFS Patient

The first job of the OMFS DCT is not to know everything—it is to recognise the sick patient, provide safe initial management, and know when to ask for help.

#acutely-unwell#sepsis#airway#haemorrhage#deteriorating-patient#escalation#on-call#patient-safety
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

Deterioration on OMFS wards is often due to airway compromise from facial swelling, sepsis from odontogenic infections, or post-operative bleeding. As the DCT, you’ll usually be first to review. Your job is not to diagnose every cause — it’s to recognise illness, start ABCDE, and escalate. Early escalation is good judgement, not weakness.

Golden Rule

Treat the patient, not the numbers. But never ignore the numbers. If NEWS2 ≥5, or you are worried, escalate. You can always de-escalate later.

⚠ 1. Recognising Deterioration: NEWS2 + OMFS Red Flags

OMFS-specific red flags that trump NEWS2:

Stridor, drooling, new voice change, rapidly expanding neck swelling, saturations <92% on air, GCS drop ≥2, new proptosis/diplopia.

3. The Big Three OMFS Emergencies

A. Airway Compromise

Causes: Ludwig’s angina, post-op haematoma, angioedema, trauma.

Actions: O2, sit up 45°, nebulised adrenaline 5ml 1:1000 if stridor, call anaesthetics + OMFS reg +theatres. Do NOT lie flat. Do NOT attempt to examine throat if high risk.

B. Sepsis

Think Sepsis 6 within 1 hour: O2, blood cultures, lactate/VBG, IV antibiotics per local OMFSguidelines, IV fluids, urine output, senior review.

Common OMFS sources: odontogenic, necrotising fasciitis, aspiration pneumonia.

C. Haemorrhage

Post-op neck: remove skin clips, evacuate clot at bedside if airway threatened, call theatres.

Intra-oral: pressure, tranexamic acid 1g IV, packing. Activate major haemorrhage protocol if>150ml/min or haemodynamic instability.

New confusion in elderly = hypoxia, sepsis, or bleed until proven otherwise

DCT Checklist: Deteriorating Patient

✓A: Airway patent? Adjuncts needed?
✓B: RR, SpO2, O2 on?
✓C: HR, BP, IV access, fluids running?
✓D: GCS, glucose checked?
✓E: Temp, exposed surgical site?
✓NEWS2 calculated and handed over
✓Bloods: FBC, U&E, CRP, lactate, clotting, G&S
✓ECG if tachycardia/chest pain
✓Seniors informed: who, when, what said
✓Documentation timed and signed

If you’re worried at the bedside, you’re already late. Worried = escalate

“Patient stable for now” is a dangerous phrase in facial swelling — document time of nextplanned review

In post-op neck haematoma, opening the wound at the bedside can be life-saving. If in doubt,cut the sutures

Lactate >2 in OMFS infection = assume deep neck space involvement until proven otherwise

Key Takeaways

NEWS2 + OMFS red flags — use both. A score of 4 with stridor still needs anaesthetics now

ABCDE + call for help — you can do both simultaneously. Never assess alone if worried

Airway, sepsis, bleeding — the three OMFS killers. Know the immediate steps for each

SBAR when you escalate — Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation. Be clear

Document times — when you noticed, when you called, who you spoke to, what plan was made

Registrar Handover Prompt - SBAR

Before calling, structure it:

S: “I’m calling about Mr Smith, a 45yo day-1 post-op neck dissection, NEWS 8”

B: “He was fine 1hr ago, now stridor and neck swelling since drain removed”

A: “I think he has a post-op haematoma compromising airway. RR 28, SpO2 91% on 15L”

R: “I need you to come now and I’ve called anaesthetics. Shall I open the wound?”
