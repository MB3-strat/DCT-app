Human and Animal Bite Injuries

Bite wounds may look small on the surface, but they are contaminated injuries with a high risk of infection. Respect every bite, irrigate it thoroughly and never underestimate what lies beneath the skin.

Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Module Matters

Bite injuries are common OMFS/ED presentations. Human and animal mouths contain polymicrobial flora with high pathogenic potential. Human bites have higher infection rates than dog bites due to Eikenella, Strep, Staph, anaerobes, and viral risk. Cat bites cause deep puncture wounds with Pasteurella multocida → rapid cellulitis/septic arthritis. Small skin wounds often hide significant deep tissue damage, devitalised tissue, or foreign bodies like teeth. Delayed presentation, inadequate irrigation, and premature closure cause abscess, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, and systemic infection. Rabies, tetanus, hepatitis B/C, and HIV must be considered. Your role: assess thoroughly, irrigate aggressively, debride devitalised tissue, decide on closure vs delayed closure, give prophylaxis, and safety-net.

Golden Rule

Every bite is contaminated. Irrigation and debridement are more important than antibiotics. Never close a high-risk bite primarily without senior discussion.

Classification By Source:

Human: Occlusional bites, clenched-fist injuries/“fight bite” over MCPJ, love bites, paediatric. Highest infection rate 10–50%. Risk: Eikenella corrodens, Strep, Staph aureus, anaerobes, Hep B/C, HIV, HSV.

Dog: Crush + tear + puncture. Large breeds = tissue loss. Risk: Pasteurella, Staph, Strep, Capnocytophaga, anaerobes. Rabies risk depends on geography/animal status.

Cat: Puncture wounds. Small surface wound, deep inoculation. Pasteurella multocida → rapid progression <24h. High risk septic arthritis/osteomyelitis.

Other: Bats, monkeys, rodents. Rabies + specific organisms.

By Timing:

<6–8 hours: “Golden period” for washout. Lower infection risk.

8–24 hours: High infection risk. Delayed primary closure or leave open.

24 hours: Infected by definition. Do not close primarily.

Initial Assessment – ABCDE

Most isolated bites are not life-threatening, but assess airway if facial/neck bites, breathing if chest bites, circulation if major vessel involved. Check for shock in extensive bites or paediatrics.

History

Mechanism: Provoked/unprovoked, domestic/wild/stray animal, known human, circumstances. Unprovoked animal attack raises rabies concern.

Time: Exact time of injury — determines closure options and prophylaxis windows.

Animal status: Vaccinated, behaviour, quarantined, location for bats.

Patient factors: Diabetes, immunosuppression, asplenia, liver disease, prosthetic joints, PVD — all increase infection risk.

First aid: Was wound washed? Antibiotics already given?

Tetanus: Last dose, number of doses.

BBV risk: Human bites — Hep B status of patient + assailant, HIV risk, last sexual health screen.

Examination

General: Temp, pulse, BP. Sepsis? Distressed child?

Wound: Site, size, depth, shape, edges. Puncture vs laceration vs avulsion. Crushing? Devitalised tissue?

Explore: Under LA and good light. Must see base. Assess through full range of movement — tendon injuries hidden when static. Look for foreign bodies: tooth fragments, debris.

Deep structures: Tendon — loss of function, tenosynovitis. Joint — penetrate capsule? Bone — fracture, tooth marks on bone. Nerve — sensation distal. Vessel — cap refill, Doppler. Cartilage — ear/nose lacerations.

Infection: Erythema, warmth, swelling, purulent discharge, lymphangitis, lymphadenopathy, fever, spreading cellulitis.

Hand bites: Always assume joint/tendon involvement until proven otherwise. “Fight bite” over MCPJ = septic arthritis risk. XR for foreign body and air in joint.

Red Flags 🚩

Urgent senior/theatre if: deep puncture over joint/tendon/bone, crush injury with devitalised tissue, signs of infection, systemic illness, functional deficit, nerve/artery injury, facial bites with cosmetic/functional concern, ear/nose cartilage involvement, child, immunocompromised, delayed presentation >24h, human bite to hand, suspected fracture.

Investigations

X-ray: If suspected fracture, joint penetration, foreign body, tooth fragment. Clenched-fist injury — XR MCPJ.

USS: Consider for deep collection/abscess.

CT: If extensive facial bite, bony involvement, deep neck spaces.

Bloods: FBC, CRP, U&E if infected/systemically unwell, admission likely. Blood cultures if febrile.

Swabs: Take from deep tissue/pus for MC&S before antibiotics if infected. Routine swab of fresh wound low yield.

Viral: Human bite — consider Hep B, Hep C, HIV serology baseline + follow-up per local protocol. Risk assess for PEP.

Management

Step 1 – Irrigation: Copious irrigation is most important. Use sterile saline under pressure — 19G needle + 50ml syringe, or pulse lavage. Minimum 250–500ml per wound. More for deep/contaminated.

Step 2 – Debridement: Excise all devitalised tissue, clot, foreign material. Explore to base. Extend wound if needed for access. Do not leave necrotic tissue.

Step 3 – Closure: Decision depends on time, site, contamination, species.

Primary closure: Clean, <8h old, face/scalp for cosmesis, not hands/feet/joints, after thorough washout. Always discuss facial bites with senior.

Delayed primary closure: 3–5 days, high-risk wounds after initial washout + packing.

Healing by secondary intention: Heavily contaminated, >24h old, puncture wounds, hands, feet, immunocompromised. Pack and review.

Step 4 – Antibiotics: Prophylaxis indicated for most bite wounds. Cover Pasteurella, Staph, Strep, anaerobes. Follow local guidelines. Typical: co-amoxiclav PO/IV. Penicillin allergy: doxycycline + metronidazole or clindamycin + ciprofloxacin per local policy. Duration: 3–5 days prophylaxis, 7–14 days if infected.

Step 5 – Tetanus: Assess status. Clean wound + fully immunised <10y ago = none. Tetanus-prone wound or >10y = booster. If unknown/incomplete, give vaccine ± immunoglobulin per national guidance.

Step 6 – Rabies: Risk assess per UKHSA algorithm. Bite from bat, or terrestrial mammal in endemic country. Post-exposure prophylaxis = vaccine ± HRIG depending on category. Discuss with virology/ID. Do not delay.

Step 7 – BBV: Human bites. Risk assess for Hep B PEP/vaccine, Hep C follow-up, HIV PEP if high-risk. Baseline serology for patient. Document discussion.

Step 8 – Elevation: Especially hand injuries. Reduces oedema/infection.

Step 9 – Follow-up: Review 24–48h for high-risk wounds. Hands need hand surgery review. Facial wounds need OMFS/plastics follow-up.

Specific Scenarios

Human bite to hand / Fight bite: Always assume joint penetration. XR, washout in theatre, splint, IV antibiotics, admit. High risk septic arthritis.

Cat bite: Early antibiotics even if looks clean. Pasteurella progresses rapidly. Low threshold for admission.

Facial bite: Cosmetic + functional importance. Washout + delayed primary closure common. Discuss with senior/plastics. Avoid closing if infected.

Ear/nose: Cartilage involved = high infection risk. Debride carefully, avoid suturing through cartilage, bolsters, ENT/OMFS input.

Child: Non-accidental injury must be considered. Paediatric safeguarding if concerns. Dog bites to face common.

Admission Criteria

Systemic infection, spreading cellulitis, deep structure involvement, need for GA washout, facial bite requiring specialist closure, immunocompromised, failed outpatient treatment, poor compliance, safeguarding concerns.

Key Takeaways

All bites are contaminated — irrigate aggressively and debride devitalised tissue

Do not close high-risk wounds primarily — hands, feet, punctures, >8–24h, infected, immunocompromised

Consider tetanus, rabies, Hep B/C, HIV — risk assess every bite

Low threshold for senior review/theatre — especially hands, face, joints, children

Registrar Handover Prompt

Structure: species, time, site, depth, structures, infection, neurovascular, irrigation done, XR, tetanus/rabies/BBV, plan.

“Dog bite 2h ago to L cheek, 3cm laceration, no deep structure involved, neurovascular intact. Irrigated 500ml saline, no foreign body. Tetanus up to date, dog vaccinated UK, no rabies risk. Plan: delayed primary closure in AM, co-amoxiclav, OMFS clinic 48h.”

Red flag: “Human fight bite to R hand MCPJ 12h ago. Swelling, pain on movement, XR shows air in joint. For theatre washout, IV co-amoxiclav, elevate, hand surgery referral.”

Don’t close high-risk wounds — hands, punctures, late, dirty

Prophylaxis — antibiotics + tetanus + consider rabies/BBV

If you do these four, you prevent most complications.
