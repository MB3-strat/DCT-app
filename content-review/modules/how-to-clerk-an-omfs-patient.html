How to Clerk an OMFS Patient

A good clerking tells the patient's story, demonstrates your clinical reasoning and allows any member of the team to safely continue their care.

#clerking#documentation#assessment#patient-safety#airway#diagnosis#handover#professionalism
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

Clerking is one of the most important skills you will develop during your OMFS rotation. A structured assessment not only supports accurate diagnosis and management but also provides a clear record of your clinical reasoning. Well-documented clerkings improve communication, facilitate safe handovers and protect both patients and clinicians. Your role is not simply to document findings but to identify the patient's immediate problems, recognise red flags and formulate an initial management plan.

Golden Rule

Use the same structured clerking format for every patient. Tailor your history and examination to the presenting complaint while never overlooking the airway or vital signs.

The OMFS Clerking Framework

Patient Details, 2. Presenting Complaint, 3. History of Presenting Complaint, 4. Medical History, 5. Drug History, 6. Allergies, 7. Social History, 8. Dental History, 9. Examination, 10. Investigations, 11. Differential Diagnosis, 12. Impression, 13. Management Plan. If you always follow the same format, you are far less likely to omit important information.

Examination Must-Haves

Always begin with: General Assessment — NEWS2, temperature, hydration, distress.

Extraoral: facial symmetry, swelling, bruising, lacerations, skin changes, tenderness, fluctuance, bony step, lymph nodes, cranial nerves, eye movements, visual acuity if trauma.

Intraoral: oral hygiene, dentition, soft tissues, floor of mouth, tongue, palate, uvula, occlusion, mouth opening in mm, source tooth.

Never forget: airway assessment, maximum inter-incisal opening in infection/trauma.

⚠ Red Flags ⚠️

Airway: stridor, drooling, trismus <15mm, floor of mouth elevation, voice change

Trauma: malocclusion, open fracture, step deformity, infraorbital paraesthesia

DCT Clerking Checklist

Before leaving the patient, confirm documented:

✓Presenting complaint
✓Full history
✓Medical history
✓Drug history
✓Allergies
✓Social history
✓Dental history
✓Observations
✓Complete examination
✓Relevant investigations
✓Differential diagnosis
✓Impression
✓Management plan
✓Senior discussion

Consultant Pearls 💡

Relevant negatives are as important as positive findings. Documenting no dysphagia, no voice change, no floor of mouth elevation is valuable

Never copy previous notes without confirming findings yourself

If you think a patient is deteriorating, document the change and reassess rather than relying on initial clerking

Key Takeaways

Use the same structured format for every patient — consistency prevents omissions

Summarise clinical reasoning with differential diagnosis and impression

A concise, well-organised clerking improves patient safety and demonstrates professionalism

Registrar Handover Prompt

Before calling, know: airway status, observations, key findings, imaging, bloods, working diagnosis, initial management, specific question. Aim to summarise in under one minute.
