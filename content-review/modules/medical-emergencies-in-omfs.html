Medical Emergencies in OMFS

Medical emergencies are uncommon, but when they occur there is no time to open a textbook. Preparation, teamwork and a structured approach save lives.

Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Module Matters

OMFS patients can have medical emergencies unrelated to surgery: vasovagal syncope in clinic, anaphylaxis to LA/antibiotics, seizures, hypoglycaemia, MI, cardiac arrest. You may be first on scene in clinic, ward, or theatre. There is no time to revise — preparation + ABCDE + teamwork saves lives. Your role: recognise, initiate immediate management, call for help early, and communicate clearly. The sickest patient is not always surgical.

Apply ABCDE approach to any collapsed/unwell OMFS patient

Recognise and initiate management of common emergencies: syncope, anaphylaxis, seizure, hypoglycaemia, chest pain, cardiac arrest, LAST

Golden Rule

Treat what you find. If airway/breathing/circulation is compromised, act now and get help — don’t wait for a diagnosis.

Universal Approach – ABCDE + DRSABCD

Danger – ensure scene safe

Response – AVPU. If unresponsive → shout for help + 2222

Send for help – call crash team early

Airway – open with head tilt/chin lift or jaw thrust if C-spine concern. Suction. Consider adjuncts

Breathing – look/listen/feel. RR, SpO2, work of breathing. O2 15L non-rebreather if hypoxic. BVM if not breathing adequately

Circulation – pulse, BP, CRT, bleeding. IV access. Fluid challenge if hypotensive.

Disability – GCS, pupils, BM. Treat hypoglycaemia

Exposure – temp, rashes, swelling. Don’t miss anaphylaxis

Common OMFS Medical Emergencies

1. Vasovagal Syncope / Faint

Trigger: Pain, sight of blood, LA injection, anxiety.

Signs: Pallor, sweating, nausea, bradycardia, hypotension, transient LOC.

Management: Lie flat, legs elevated. O2 if needed. Recovery in seconds–minutes. Check BM, HR, BP. Don’t sit up too quickly. Reassure. If LOC prolonged >1min or not recovering → treat as collapse, call help.

2. Anaphylaxis

Trigger: Antibiotics, LA, latex, chlorhexidine.

Signs: ABC problem. Rapid onset. Airway: swelling, hoarseness. Breathing: wheeze, stridor. Circulation: hypotension, tachycardia. Skin: urticaria, flushing.

Management – Resus Council UK:

Call for help. 2222.

Remove trigger. Lie flat, legs up unless breathing difficulty.

Adrenaline IM 0.5mg 1:1000 anterolateral thigh. Repeat 5min if no improvement.

O2 15L, IV access, fluid bolus 500–1000ml.

Chlorphenamine 10mg + Hydrocortisone 200mg IV/IM.

If airway threatened → anaesthetics + prepare for difficult airway. Biphasic reactions can occur — observe 6–12h.

3. Local Anaesthetic Systemic Toxicity LAST

Trigger: Inadvertent IV injection, excessive dose.

Early: Perioral tingling, metallic taste, tinnitus, agitation, slurred speech.

Late: Seizures, ↓GCS, arrhythmias, CV collapse.

Management: Stop injecting. Call for help + anaesthetics. ABCDE. Intralipid 20%: 1.5ml/kg bolus over 1min, then infusion 15ml/kg/hr. CPR if arrest — prolonged efforts may be needed. AAGBI LAST guideline.

4. Seizure

Common: Epilepsy, alcohol withdrawal, hypoglycaemia, hypoxia.

Management: Safe environment, time seizure, O2, BM. Do not restrain. Recovery position post-ictal. If >5min or repeated → status: benzodiazepine per local protocol: buccal midazolam 10mg or IV lorazepam 4mg. Call anaesthetics. Check glucose, U&E, calcium.

5. Hypoglycaemia

Risk: Diabetics, fasting pre-op, alcoholics.

Signs: Sweating, agitation, confusion, seizures, coma. BM <4.0 mmol/L.

Management: If conscious: 15–20g fast-acting glucose orally. If unconscious: IV 10% glucose 150–200ml or IM glucagon 1mg. Recheck BM 10min. Find cause.

6. Chest Pain / ACS

Consider: MI, angina, PE, aortic dissection.

Signs: Crushing chest pain, radiation to arm/jaw, SOB, nausea, sweating.

Management: Call for help. Sit up. O2 if hypoxic. GTN spray if BP OK. Aspirin 300mg chewed. ECG + troponin. If STEMI → cath lab. If arrest → ALS.

7. Cardiac Arrest Management – ALS:

Confirm unresponsive + not breathing normally. Call 2222 + start CPR.

30 compressions : 2 breaths, rate 100–120/min, depth 5–6cm.

Attach defibrillator — shock if VF/VT.

Adrenaline 1mg IV every 3–5min. Amiodarone 300mg after 3rd shock.

Reversible causes: 4Hs + 4Ts. Hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo/hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, tamponade, tension pneumothorax, toxins, thrombosis.

Emergency Drugs – Know Location

Adrenaline 1:1000 IM, Atropine, Glucagon, Glucose 10%, Hydrocortisone, Chlorphenamine, Midazolam, Aspirin, GTN, Salbutamol neb, Intralipid 20%. Know your crash trolley.

Red Flags 🚨

Call for senior help immediately if: unresponsive, airway noise, RR <8 or >30, SpO2 <90% on O2, HR <40 or >130, SBP <90, GCS drop ≥2, seizure >5min, chest pain, suspected anaphylaxis, any LAST signs.

Registrar Handover Prompt – SBAR

Situation: “45F dental extraction collapsed in clinic, unresponsive.”

Background: “Fit and well, LA lidocaine given 5min ago.”

Assessment: “A patent, B RR 8, SpO2 88% on air, C HR 130, BP 85/50, D GCS 9, E rash noted. Impression: anaphylaxis.”

Recommendation: “Given adrenaline 0.5mg IM, O2 15L, IV access. Need anaesthetics + crash team now.”

Defib — attach ASAP

Drugs — adrenaline if anaphylaxis/arrest, glucose if low BM
