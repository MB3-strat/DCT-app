Theatre Survival for the OMFS DCT

Theatre is not just where operations happen—it is where surgeons are trained. Preparation, anticipation and teamwork are as important as technical skill.

#theatre#scrubbing#assisting#WHO-checklist#surgical-skills#professionalism#learning
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

For many new DCTs, theatre is an unfamiliar environment with its own language, routines, etiquette and expectations. It can feel intimidating, but it is also where some of the most valuable learning in OMFS takes place. A good assistant makes every operation safer, more efficient and more enjoyable. Your role is not passive—you are an integral part of the surgical team. By understanding the procedure, anticipating the next step and maintaining situational awareness, you contribute directly to patient safety and your own surgical development.

Golden Rule

The best assistant is the one the surgeon hardly has to ask. Anticipation, preparation and communication are the hallmarks of an excellent theatre assistant.

Core Content

Before the Theatre List

Review: operating list, indication for each procedure, relevant imaging, clinic letters, bloods, consent forms. Ask: Why is this operation being performed? What anatomy is at risk? What complications might occur? What instruments are likely to be required? 15 minutes reading the evening before improves learning more than reading afterwards.

Arriving in Theatre

Aim to arrive before the consultant. Introduce yourself to: theatre nurses, ODPs, anaesthetists, recovery staff, students. Learn names. Good teamwork starts with communication.

Your Responsibilities Before First Patient

Check:

✓Operating list correct
✓Imaging available
✓Consent completed
✓Notes present
✓Bloods available
✓Implants/equipment available
✓You understand the procedure.

Understanding the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist

Sign In — before anaesthesia: patient ID, procedure, consent, allergies, airway, antibiotics, anticipated risks.

Time Out — before incision: correct patient/procedure/site, team introductions, imaging, equipment, critical steps. Every team member can speak up.

Sign Out — before patient leaves: procedure performed, counts correct, specimens labelled, equipment issues, post-op plan.

Scrubbing, Gowning and Gloving

Maintain strict aseptic technique. Remove jewellery, nails short/no varnish, surgical hand prep per local policy, hands above elbows after scrubbing, avoid non-sterile surfaces. If sterility broken, inform team and re-scrub. Patient safety before embarrassment.

How to Be an Excellent Assistant

Good assistants: hold retractors steadily, maintain tissue tension, keep field visible, suction without obstructing, follow the operation, anticipate next instrument, protect soft tissues. Poor assistance makes simple procedures difficult.

Anticipating the Operation

For surgical extraction: LA → incision → flap → bone removal → tooth sectioning → delivery → debridement → irrigation → haemostasis → closure. Understanding sequence lets you anticipate instruments and improve flow.

Common OMFS Theatre Procedures

During DCT year you’ll likely assist: surgical removal of impacted teeth, I&D of odontogenic infections, ORIF facial fractures, dentoalveolar trauma, soft tissue biopsies, excision of oral lesions, neck dissections, skin lesion excisions, salivary gland surgery, tracheostomy.

After the Operation

Before leaving theatre ensure: operation note completed, drug chart updated, post-op instructions documented, specimens sent, imaging reviewed if required, relatives updated, follow-up arranged. Never assume someone else has completed these.

Red Flags ⚠️

In theatre: unlabelled specimen, missing swab/instrument, patient moved before Time Out, allergy not checked, wrong site marking, sterility breach not declared. Speak up — it’s your responsibility.

DCT Theatre Checklist

Before list:

✓Read about procedures
✓Review imaging
✓Check consent
✓Know diagnosis
✓Understand operation.

During:

✓Maintain sterility
✓Retract effectively
✓Anticipate instruments
✓Protect tissues
✓Observe anatomy.

After:

✓Documentation complete
✓Post-op plan confirmed
✓Follow-up arranged
✓Reflect on learning.

Respect tissues, maintain sterility, communicate with the team

Every operation is an opportunity to improve technical knowledge, anatomy, and judgement

Registrar Handover Prompt

After theatre, update your registrar: cases done, complications, drains, antibiotics, post-op instructions, concerns, plans for review. Be specific: “Patient returned to ward, obs stable, drain 20ml, plan review AM with consultant.”
