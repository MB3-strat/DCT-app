Common Sutures, Needles and Local Anaesthetic in OMFS

Choosing the correct suture or local anaesthetic is not about memorising names—it is about understanding tissue, healing and the procedure you are performing.

Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Module Matters

As a new OMFS DCT you’ll use LA and sutures daily — clinic, ED, MOS lists, theatre. Consultants don’t expect you to know every product day one. They do expect you to understand principles and use materials safely: which LA to choose, when vasoconstrictors are used/cautioned, suture choice for tissue, needle types, safe injection/suturing, and common pitfalls. Remember: The best closure begins before the first stitch. Gentle tissue handling, good haemostasis and tension-free wound edges matter more than the suture itself.

Golden Rule

Always know what you are injecting, how much you have given and why you have chosen it. Never inject a cartridge without checking its contents.

Local Anaesthetic Agents

Vasoconstrictors – Adrenaline

Benefits: ↓bleeding, ↑duration, ↓systemic absorption, improves operative field.

Caution: significant CVD, uncontrolled arrhythmias, unstable angina, MI <6 months, phaeochromocytoma, severe hyperthyroidism. Use clinical judgement + local guidance.

Maximum Recommended Doses – Calculate Every Time

Depends on: agent, patient weight, age/frailty, hepatic function, comorbidities. Never rely on cartridge number alone. If unsure, calculate or ask.

Key examples — always check BNF/local protocol:

Lidocaine + adrenaline: 7 mg/kg. 2.2ml cartridge = 44mg → 70kg adult max ∼11 cartridges.

Articaine + adrenaline: 7 mg/kg. 1.7ml cartridge = 68mg → 70kg adult max ∼7 cartridges.

Mepivacaine plain: 4.4 mg/kg → 70kg adult max ∼6 cartridges.

Bupivacaine: 2 mg/kg.

In children/elderly reduce. Max doses lower in liver disease.

✓Correct patient
✓Correct procedure

Injection Principles

Explain, position patient, dry mucosa, topical if appropriate, stretch tissues, inject slowly, aspirate before injecting, observe afterwards. Slow injections = less painful + ↓ intravascular risk.

Common LA Techniques

Become familiar with: buccal infiltration, palatal infiltration, IANB, long buccal, mental, infraorbital, greater palatine, nasopalatine blocks. Understand anatomy before independent practice.

Local Anaesthetic Toxicity – Recognise It

Rare but emergency. Early: circumoral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus, dizziness, agitation, slurred speech. Severe: seizures, ↓GCS, arrhythmias, arrest. Stop injecting, call for help, follow local LAST protocol — Intralipid 20%.

Common Suture Materials

Suture Sizes

Common OMFS: 3-0, 4-0, 5-0, 6-0. Rule: face deserves finer sutures than scalp. More zeros = finer. 6-0 finer than 3-0.

Needle Types

Reverse Cutting: Commonest in OMFS. Excellent for skin, strong, ↓tissue tearing.

Cutting: Tough tissues, oral mucosa.

Taper / Round-bodied: Delicate soft tissues, muscle. Less used for skin.

Basic Suturing Principles

Aim: gentle tissue handling, accurate wound edge approximation, minimal tension, good haemostasis, even spacing. Suture should approximate — not strangulate — tissue.

Common Techniques

Confident with: simple interrupted, horizontal mattress, vertical mattress, figure-of-eight, continuous, subcuticular. Simple interrupted = foundation.

Instrument Handling

Needle holder dominant hand, tissue forceps non-dominant. Never grasp needle with forceps if avoidable. Hold needle 2/3 from tip.

Knot Tying: Master instrument ties, two-handed, one-handed. Secure knot > elegant knot.

DCT Checklist: If Preparing for Dentoalveolar Procedure

✓LA: Lidocaine 2% + adrenaline unless contraindicated
✓Needle: short/long dental depending on block
✓Suture: 3-0 or 4-0 Vicryl Rapide® for intraoral
✓Instruments: needle holder, Adson forceps, suture scissors

Wound healing depends on technique more than suture brand

Clinical Pearl – The Consultant's Question

If asked: "What is the most important principle of wound closure?"

Strong answer: "Achieving accurate, tension-free tissue approximation while preserving tissue blood supply. The suture should support healing, not compensate for poor surgical technique."

Quality of closure is determined before the final knot.

DCT Survival Box 🚨

The Everyday OMFS Set-Up

For routine dentoalveolar procedure think:

LA: Lidocaine 2% + adrenaline unless contraindicated

Needle: Short or long dental depending on injection

Suture: 3-0 or 4-0 Vicryl Rapide® intraoral

Instruments: Needle holder, Adson forceps, suture scissors

Knowing this standard set-up prepares you for most routine oral surgery and helps anticipate consultant needs.

Common Mistakes ⚠️

Forgetting to calculate max dose, injecting too quickly, failing to aspirate, crushing tissues, overtightening sutures, wound tension, unnecessarily large sutures for facial skin.
