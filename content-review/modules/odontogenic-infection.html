Odontogenic Infection

Most odontogenic infections are straightforward. The skill lies in recognising the few that are not.

#odontogenic-infection#facial-swelling#abscess#cellulitis#trismus#source-control#antibiotics
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

Odontogenic infection is the commonest reason for emergency OMFS referral. Most are uncomplicated and managed with drainage + source control. A small number progress rapidly through fascial planes, causing sepsis, airway compromise, and death. Your job: assess airway, recognise sepsis, identify source, decide if source control needed, escalate early. A swollen face is not the diagnosis — it’s the symptom.

Golden Rule

Pus needs a scalpel, not just a prescription pad. Antibiotics support treatment. They rarely replace definitive source control.

Core Content

Pathophysiology

Starts with pulpal necrosis → periapical spread → perforates bone → spreads via path of least resistance. Root apex position vs muscle attachments determines fascial space involved.

Initial Assessment – ABCDE

Most have normal airway, but never assume. Specifically assess: difficulty swallowing, voice change, floor of mouth swelling, bilateral neck swelling, respiratory distress. These suggest progression beyond simple infection.

History

Onset: Hours, days, weeks? Rapid progression concerning.

Pain: Severity, constant/intermittent, worse on biting, night pain.

Dental symptoms: Toothache, broken tooth, recent extraction, pericoronitis.

Red flags: Dysphagia, odynophagia, voice change, drooling, fever, rigors, trismus.

PMH: Diabetes, steroids, chemo, immunosuppression, radiotherapy, MRONJ risk, pregnancy.

Examination

General: Temp, pulse, BP, RR, SpO2, NEWS2. Septic appearance?

Extraoral: Site/extent of swelling, skin erythema, tenderness, fluctuance, induration, cervical lymph nodes.

Mouth opening: Measure inter-incisal distance. Normal 35–50mm. <20mm = significant trismus.

Intraoral: Oral hygiene, carious teeth, tooth mobility, percussion tenderness, buccal/palatal swelling, floor of mouth, tongue position, pus discharge, sinus tracts. Identify source tooth.

Which Fascial Space?

Red Flags ⚠️

Escalate immediately: stridor, difficulty managing secretions, voice change, tongue elevation, raised floor of mouth, bilateral submandibular swelling, rapidly progressive swelling, trismus <20mm, sepsis, immunocompromised, failure to improve on antibiotics, nursing/ED concern.

Investigations

Bloods: FBC, CRP, U&E, blood cultures if febrile/septic, VBG with lactate if unwell. Normal CRP does not exclude serious infection early.

OPG: Identify source tooth, impacted teeth, periapical pathology. Does not define extent of deep infection.

Contrast CT Neck: Investigation of choice if deep space infection suspected, significant neck swelling, uncertain source, trismus/dysphagia/toxicity, considering drainage. Answers: cellulitis vs abscess, extent.

Management Algorithm

ABCDE. Is airway threatened? Yes → call reg, anaesthetics, ED senior now.

Establish source: Odontogenic, salivary, non-odontogenic?

Deep fascial space? Yes → contrast CT if safe + urgent reg discussion.

Source control: Extraction, endo, I&D. Antibiotics are adjunct, not substitute.

Admission Criteria

Consider if: airway concerns, deep space infection, significant trismus, dysphagia, sepsis, IV antibiotics needed, surgery needed, significant comorbidity, failed outpatient treatment.

Antibiotics

Indicated when: spreading cellulitis, systemic involvement, drainage not immediately achievable, significant comorbidities, deep space infection. Not routinely required for every dental abscess if drainage + source control achieved in healthy patient. Follow local guidelines.

DCT Checklist: Before You Phone Your Registrar

✓Is airway normal?
✓Is patient systemically well?
✓Which tooth is responsible?
✓Is there trismus? Document mm.
✓Is there dysphagia?
✓Is there fluctuance?
✓Is there a collection needing drainage?
✓Does patient need IV antibiotics?
✓Does patient require admission?
✓Is imaging required?

When in doubt, reassess and escalate — deterioration can be rapid

Registrar Handover Prompt

Know: airway status, NEWS2, source tooth, mouth opening, floor of mouth, dysphagia/voice change, bloods, CT findings, working diagnosis, whether source control achieved, admission need.
