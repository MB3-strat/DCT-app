Surviving the Ward Round

The best ward rounds are organised before they begin. Preparation saves time, improves patient care and allows the team to focus on clinical decision-making rather than administration.

#ward-round#organisation#documentation#MDT#patient-safety#discharge-planning#professionalism
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

The ward round is the centrepiece of inpatient care in OMFS. It is where diagnoses are reviewed, treatment plans refined, patients prepared for theatre, discharge decisions made and complications recognised. For a new DCT, ward rounds can feel intimidating. They are often fast-paced, involve multiple disciplines and require you to think, document and organise simultaneously. Your role is not simply to write notes—it is to ensure every decision made is translated into safe, effective patient care. The most successful DCTs are not those with the greatest clinical knowledge; they are the ones who are organised, anticipate what the team needs and ensure nothing is forgotten.

Golden Rule

Preparation before the ward round saves time afterwards. A consultant should spend their time making clinical decisions—not waiting for blood results to be found or scans to be opened.

Core Content

Before the Ward Round

Arrive early. Before consultant arrives:

✓Review every patient
✓Read overnight handover
✓Check nursing concerns
✓Review overnight obs
✓Check NEWS2
✓Review blood results
✓Review microbiology
✓Open all relevant imaging
✓Check theatre lists
✓Identify planned discharges
✓Speak to nurse in charge. If something changed overnight, know about it before you’re asked.

Organising Your Patient List

For each patient include: name, age, hospital number, location, diagnosis, admission date, operations performed, outstanding investigations, expected discharge date. Leave space for today's plan.

The Consultant Will Usually Ask

For every inpatient: Why are they in hospital? Which operation have they had? How many days post-op? How were they overnight? What do today's bloods show? Have they eaten and mobilised? Are they fit for discharge? What still needs to happen?

Presenting an Inpatient

Concise presentation <30 seconds: "Mr Brown is a 62-year-old, day one following I&D of left submandibular abscess with extraction LL7. Overnight afebrile, NEWS2 1, pain controlled, tolerating oral fluids. CRP fallen 186 to 112. Drain output minimal. Remains on IV antibiotics."

Writing the Ward Round Entry

Include: date and time, consultant leading, current clinical status, examination findings, investigation results, assessment, numbered management plan, your name and designation. Example:

Consultant Ward Round – Mr Smith – 08:15

Day 2 post ORIF left mandibular angle. Comfortable at rest. Afebrile. NEWS2 0. Wounds clean. Occlusion stable. Mild lower lip paraesthesia unchanged. Bloods improving.

Plan

1. Continue IV co-amoxiclav today

2. Convert to oral antibiotics tomorrow if appropriate

3. Soft diet 6 weeks

4. Physio jaw exercises

5. Repeat FBC/CRP tomorrow

6. Home tomorrow if well

The Ward Jobs List

Prioritise by urgency:

Priority 1 – Immediate: Deteriorating patient, blood transfusion, theatre consent, CT request, urgent senior review, airway concerns.

Priority 2 – Time-Critical: Prescriptions, blood requests, drain removal, micro discussion, referrals.

Priority 3 – Routine: Discharge summaries, follow-up appointments, clinic letters, updating list.

Red Flags ⚠️

Never ignore: increasing NEWS2, new dysphagia, voice change, increasing swelling, bleeding, reduced urine output, confusion, increasing O2 requirement. Escalate concerns early.

DCT Ward Round Checklist

Before leaving each patient:

✓Today's exam documented?
✓Consultant's plan written clearly?
✓Jobs added to list?
✓Nursing team know the plan?
✓Outstanding investigations?
✓For theatre?
✓Suitable for discharge?

Good organisation is a defining characteristic of an excellent OMFS DCT

Registrar Handover Prompt

After ward round, summarise for your registrar: sick patients first, new plans, new admissions, expected discharges, theatre list changes, any concerns. Use the same structure as your jobs list.
