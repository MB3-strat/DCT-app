Looking After Yourself on Call

You cannot provide safe care for patients if you do not look after yourself. Fatigue, hunger, stress and poor organisation affect clinical performance more than most trainees realise.

#wellbeing#on-call#fatigue#resilience#patient-safety#professionalism#self-care
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

Every OMFS DCT remembers their first on-call shift. It can be exciting, rewarding, exhausting and, at times, overwhelming. You will assess patients in pain, deal with emergencies at night, make important clinical decisions and manage competing priorities. No one expects you to know everything. What is expected is that you remain organised, recognise your limitations, ask for help when appropriate and continue to provide safe, compassionate care. Looking after yourself is not a luxury—it is a professional responsibility. A tired, dehydrated and stressed clinician is more likely to make mistakes.

Golden Rule

Your first responsibility is to keep patients safe. Your second responsibility is to keep yourself fit to do that. There is no benefit in "soldiering on" if you are exhausted, unwell or struggling.

Core Content

Before Your Shift

Evening before: check rota, know location, know on-call consultant/registrar, check expected theatre lists, get adequate sleep.

On the day: eat a proper meal, bring water bottle, healthy snacks, charger, notebook, pens, ensure hospital system access works. Starting a 12-hour shift hungry is rarely a good idea.

Organise Yourself Early

Within first 30 minutes:

✓Introduce yourself to nursing teams
✓Meet registrar
✓Know how to contact consultant
✓Find emergency theatre
✓Locate OMFS ward
✓Check emergency airway equipment location
✓Understand referral pathways. Familiarity reduces stress in emergencies.

Managing Multiple Jobs

Ask: "Which patient is at greatest risk if I delay?" Prioritise by clinical urgency, not bleep order. Airway always takes priority over paperwork.

The Four Categories of Work

Immediate: Airway compromise, active haemorrhage, septic patient, rapid deterioration, emergency theatre

Urgent: Facial cellulitis, mandibular fracture, facial laceration, dental trauma, review after CT

Routine: Dental pain, dry socket, ward jobs, routine reviews

Administrative: Discharge summaries, clinic letters, filing, updating lists. Complete when clinical work allows.

Looking After Your Basic Needs

During busy shift: drink water regularly, eat when you can, toilet before desperate, sit for a few minutes if safe. Small breaks improve concentration and reduce errors.

Fatigue

Affects decision-making, memory, communication, technical skills, professionalism. If re-reading notes, forgetting tasks, unusually irritable, struggling to concentrate: pause, hydrate, eat if possible, ask for support.

When to Call Your Registrar

Call early if: worried about airway, patient deteriorating, think emergency surgery needed, significant bleeding, unsure of diagnosis, feel out of depth. Registrar would rather be called early than after preventable deterioration.

Red Flags ⚠️

Personal red flags: making simple mistakes, forgetting to document, snapping at colleagues, feeling unsafe to drive home, dreading next shift. These signal you need support — speak to ES, TPD, or colleague.

DCT On-Call Checklist

Before every shift:

✓Have I eaten?
✓Do I have water?
✓Do I know who my registrar is?
✓Is my bleep working?
✓Do I know where emergency theatre is?
✓Do I have a notebook?
✓Do I know how to contact consultant?

At the End of Your Shift

Before leaving:

✓Complete urgent jobs
✓Handover outstanding tasks clearly
✓Ensure sick patients discussed
✓Check documentation
✓Return borrowed equipment
✓Thank the team. Leave work at work. Recovery between shifts is important.

Looking after yourself is a professional responsibility, not a luxury

Your career is a marathon — sleep, exercise, hobbies, family/friends sustain you

Registrar Handover Prompt

When asking for help because you’re overwhelmed: be honest. “I’m managing three referrals, one is airway risk, I’ve seen him but not started the others. Can you advise priority or come to help?” Specific requests get specific support.
