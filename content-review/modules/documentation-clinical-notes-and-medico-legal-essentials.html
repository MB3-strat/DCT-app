General Skills Foundation M5

Documentation, Clinical Notes and Medico-Legal Essentials

Good clinicians make good decisions. Great clinicians make good decisions and document them well.

#documentation#medico-legal#patient-safety#professionalism#communication#handover
Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Why This Matters

Documentation is a fundamental clinical skill. Every patient you assess, every procedure you perform, every referral you receive and every discussion you have with a senior colleague should be documented accurately. Your notes communicate with the MDT, provide a contemporaneous record, demonstrate clinical reasoning, support continuity of care, and form an important medico-legal document. Poor documentation places patients at risk. Excellent documentation protects patients, supports colleagues and reflects good clinical practice.

Golden Rule

If it is not documented, it is difficult to demonstrate that it happened.

Core Content

Principles of Good Documentation

Every clinical entry should be: accurate, objective, legible, contemporaneous, dated, timed, signed, attributable to you.

Before You Start Writing

Ask yourself: What is the clinical problem? What have I found? What am I worried about? What have I done? What happens next? If your notes answer those five questions, they are likely to be useful.

Every Clinical Entry Should Include

Patient Details: name, hospital/NHS number, DOB

Date and Time: every entry must include both. Never add retrospectively without stating it.

Your Identification: full name, grade, signature or authenticated electronic signature

Structured Clinical Note

Presenting Complaint: "Referred from ED with increasing left facial swelling and trismus."

History: duration, progression, associated symptoms, previous treatment, relevant PMH. Document important negatives: "No dysphagia, no voice change, no difficulty breathing."

Examination: NEWS2, temperature, general appearance. Extraoral: swelling, tenderness, cellulitis, cranial nerves. Intraoral: source tooth, mouth opening mm, floor of mouth, occlusion, soft tissues.

Investigations: FBC, CRP, U&Es. Imaging: "Contrast CT neck demonstrates 2.5 cm left submandibular collection with surrounding cellulitis. Airway remains patent."

Impression: "Odontogenic infection arising from LL7 involving left submandibular space without current airway compromise."

Plan: Numbered. 1. Admit under OMFS, 2. IV antibiotics per local guideline, 3. Repeat airway assessment, 4. Contrast CT reviewed, 5. Discuss with registrar, 6. Theatre for I&D if indicated.

Documenting Discussions

Whenever you discuss with a senior, document: name of senior, time, advice received, agreed plan. Example: "Discussed with Mr Smith, Consultant OMFS, 18:40. Agreed admission, IV antibiotics, overnight airway observation, list for theatre tomorrow." Never write: "Consultant aware."

Telephone Referrals

Document: time, referrer name/designation/department, clinical question, advice given, planned review. Example: "21:15 – Telephone referral from Dr Patel, ED Reg. 32M with mandibular trauma post-assault. Advice: CT facial bones, soft diet, OMFS review. Patient to be assessed in ED."

Procedure Notes

Document: indication, consent, anaesthesia, procedure performed, findings, complications, outcome, post-op instructions, follow-up. Example: "Extraction LL8 under LA. Written informed consent. Time-out completed. IAN + long buccal blocks 2.2ml 2% lidocaine 1:80,000 adrenaline. LL8 elevated and delivered without complication. Socket curetted, irrigated saline. Haemostasis achieved. PO instructions given verbally and written. Patient discharged with analgesia and follow-up."

Relevant Negative Findings

Documenting important negatives shows you actively assessed for serious pathology. Facial swelling: no dysphagia, no stridor, no floor of mouth elevation, no voice change. Trauma: no malocclusion, no diplopia, no infraorbital paraesthesia, no CSF rhinorrhoea. Oral lesion: no induration, no cervical lymphadenopathy, no weight loss.

Writing Good Management Plans

Avoid: "Continue treatment." Write: 1. Repeat obs Q4H, 2. Continue IV antibiotics per guideline, 3. Soft diet, 4. Consultant review AM, 5. Repeat CRP AM, 6. Escalate immediately if airway symptoms develop. Specific plans improve safety.

Red Flags ⚠️

Documentation errors: no date/time, missing observations, no airway assessment, no working diagnosis, no management plan, no senior discussion, copying previous entries without confirming, unexplained abbreviations, recording assumptions as facts.

DCT Documentation Checklist

Before leaving the patient, confirm documented:

✓Date and time
✓Presenting complaint
✓Relevant history
✓Observations
✓Examination findings
✓Relevant negatives
✓Investigation results
✓Working diagnosis
✓Numbered management plan
✓Senior discussion
✓Safety-netting
✓Your name and grade

If something worries you, document it and act on it

The quality of your notes is often a reflection of the quality of your assessment

Clear, concise notes make ward rounds, handovers and overnight reviews significantly easier

Document discussions with seniors in sufficient detail

Good documentation is an essential clinical skill and integral part of safe patient care

Registrar Handover Prompt

When presenting notes, ensure they answer: clinical problem, findings, worries, actions taken, next steps. If notes are clear, verbal handover will be clear.
