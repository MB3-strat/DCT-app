👁️ ALGORITHM Emergency T07

Retrobulbar Haemorrhage — Sight-Saving

Vision loss within 60–90 min if untreated. Bedside decompression.

Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.
1

Suspect orbital compartment syndrome in trauma with severe pain, proptosis, tense orbit, reduced visual acuity, RAPD or ophthalmoplegia.

2

Call immediately: OMFS registrar, ophthalmology and anaesthetic support if required.

3

Assess and document vision, pupils, RAPD and eye movements. Repeat frequently.

4

Elevate head to around 30 degrees and avoid unnecessary pressure on the eye.

5

Do not wait for CT if orbital compartment syndrome is strongly suspected clinically.

6

Prepare for emergency lateral canthotomy and inferior cantholysis if indicated and senior help is not immediately available.

Visual acuity checked in each eye
Pupils and RAPD checked
Eye movements and diplopia checked
Proptosis / globe position assessed
Tense orbit or rock-hard eyelids considered
Registrar called immediately
Ophthalmology called immediately
CT not allowed to delay sight-saving decompression

Escalation

Lead with vision when calling: acuity, RAPD, proptosis, tense orbit, pain on movement and suspected orbital compartment syndrome.
