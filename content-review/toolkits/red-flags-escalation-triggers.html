Red Flags & Escalation Triggers

Four quick-lookup groups — pale flap, milky drain, sudden desaturation: what it means and whether to call.

Educational aid only. This is a personal reference and survival resource. It does not replace senior clinical advice, local trust policies, professional judgment, or emergency escalation procedures.

Call senior immediately if…

Airway compromise
Tracheostomy obstruction/displacement
Any uncertainty about a laryngectomy airway
Significant neck swelling
Active haemorrhage
Suspected free-flap compromise
Sudden loss/change of flap Doppler
Rapidly increasing flap swelling
Haemodynamic instability
Significant respiratory deterioration
Suspected sepsis
Major wound breakdown
Suspected pharyngocutaneous/salivary fistula
High-output or suspected chyle leak
Sudden neurological deterioration
Any patient who looks significantly worse than earlier

Escalation

If you are worried enough to think 'I wonder if I should call the registrar', call the registrar.
